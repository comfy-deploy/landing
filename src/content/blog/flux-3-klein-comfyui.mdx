FLUX 3 Image Klein is the compact, open-weights model in the FLUX 3 family from Black Forest Labs. It has 5.6 billion parameters, and it runs natively in ComfyUI. Klein keeps the layout controls of the larger FLUX 3 Image model, including bounding boxes, in a size that fits much smaller GPUs.

## What is FLUX 3 Image Klein?

According to the ComfyUI team, Klein draws on what Black Forest Labs learned across the image, video, and audio models in the FLUX 3 family. Black Forest Labs says the release includes:

- **Base.** The full undistilled checkpoint, intended for fine-tuning, further training, and research.
- **Distilled.** A step-distilled variant aimed at fast generation. In the ComfyUI repository on Hugging Face, base files carry a `-base` suffix and distilled files do not.
- **Quantized builds.** FP8, INT8, and NVFP4 versions made with NVIDIA for smaller and faster inference.

You can read the full announcement on the [ComfyUI blog](https://blog.comfy.org/p/flux-3-image-klein-in-comfyui-56b).

The open weights are released under the FLUX Kommunity License, according to Black Forest Labs' blog and the Hugging Face model card. Black Forest Labs sells self-hosting and commercial licenses on its [open weights page](https://bfl.ai/open-weights). VentureBeat reports that companies under $5M in annual revenue can use the model commercially for free. Read the license text yourself before you ship outputs or a product built on the model.

## Key features

The ComfyUI announcement lists these capabilities:

- **Native 4K output.** Images are generated at 4K, not upscaled to it.
- **Prompt upsampler.** A separate model expands a short prompt into a detailed one before generation.
- **Multi-reference generation and editing.** You can feed the model several reference images, and subjects stay recognizable across edits and inputs.
- **Bounding boxes.** You mark regions on the canvas to set composition and layout before the model fills them in.
- **Text rendering and editing.** The model writes text into an image and edits text that is already there.
- **Reference-based style transfer.** It takes the look of a reference while keeping the content and composition you asked for.

## Speed on consumer GPUs

Black Forest Labs published timings for the base model at 50 steps. For 1024x1024 text-to-image with the INT8 build, it reports 12.5 seconds per image on an RTX 5090 and 34.0 seconds on an RTX 5070.

The distilled model needs far fewer steps. ComfyUI reports that pairing INT8 ConvRot quantization with the step-distilled Klein model makes 1024x1024 text-to-image generation 4.3x to 9.3x faster than a BF16, 50-step baseline. According to ComfyUI, an image that took 71 seconds on an RTX 5070 now takes under 8, and on an RTX 4070 the time drops from 78 seconds to under 9. These are vendor-reported figures, so measure your own graph before you plan capacity around them.

## FLUX 3 Image Klein vs Qwen-Image 2.1

Both models are open-weight and aimed at generation plus reference-based editing, so they are natural rivals. In Black Forest Labs' internal evaluations, FLUX 3 Image Klein ranks first among the compared models in text-to-image, multi-reference generation, and image editing. ComfyUI's summary of those evaluations places it above Qwen-Image 2.1 in text-to-image and multi-reference generation. Black Forest Labs also reports gains of 91 Elo in text-to-image and 70 Elo in editing over FLUX.2 [klein] 9B. Treat these as a starting point and not a verdict, since the evaluations are not independent.

The practical differences from the published specs:

- **Size.** FLUX 3 Image Klein is 5.6B parameters. Qwen-Image 2.1 is 7B, and Krea 2 is 12.9B.
- **Layout control.** FLUX 3 Image Klein supports bounding boxes for placing elements.
- **Transparency.** Qwen-Image 2.1 outputs a real alpha channel, which the FLUX 3 Image Klein announcement does not mention. Choose Qwen-Image 2.1 if you need cutouts.
- **Fine-tuning.** FLUX 3 Image Klein ships an undistilled base checkpoint for training.

If you rely on LoRAs trained for earlier Flux or Qwen releases, expect to retrain, because they were built for different architectures.

## How to run FLUX 3 Image Klein in the cloud

The model files in the distilled template add up to about 18 GB: 5.8 GB for the INT8 checkpoint, 4.9 GB for the text encoder, 6.7 GB for the prompt upsampler, and a small VAE. A 24 GB card such as an L4 or A10G may run it, since ComfyUI can unload the upsampler after it finishes, but that leaves little headroom. We suggest an L40S (48 GB) as a starting point. It keeps all models loaded, leaves room for higher resolutions and reference images, and fits the 11.1 GB BF16 checkpoint that the template recommends for cards with more than 24 GB. For the base model, 4K output, or many references, an A100 or H100 gives more room. This is a suggestion based on file sizes. We have not benchmarked Klein ourselves.
